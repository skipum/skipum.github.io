How we calculate wasteful spend

A look inside the rules engine that finds the dollars hiding in your shipping invoices — and how it gets sharper the more you ship.

Intelligent Logistics Team · April 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Most shippers know they're overpaying. They just can't prove where, by how much, or what to do about it. That's the problem Intelligent Logistics' wasteful-spend engine was built to solve — and it does it without asking you to integrate anything new.

The starting premise

A package that arrived on time and intact still might have cost too much. Wasteful spend isn't about service failure — it's about the gap between what you paid and what you needed to pay. We organize that gap into four buckets:

  • Service mismatch — air when ground was fine; priority when 2-day was fine; signature required where it wasn't.
  • Avoidable surcharges — address corrections, residential when it should have been commercial, oversize miscoding, fuel adjustments on the wrong base.
  • Discount drift — contracted rates that aren't being applied because the carrier classified the shipment differently than your tariff expects.
  • Behavioral patterns — repeat overnight shipments to the same lane, single-piece air shipments that could be batched, etc.

The engine, simplified

Every shipment we ingest passes through a rule pipeline. Each rule answers a single yes/no question — was there a cheaper version of this shipment that would have met the SLA? — and, if yes, attaches a dollar value, a confidence score, and a rationale.

The rules fall into three layers:

  1. Universal rules apply to every shipper: an overnight shipment delivered before 5pm to a destination that the same carrier reaches in 2 days for ground rates is flagged.
  2. Tariff-aware rules compare your shipment to your specific contracted rates with each carrier — including DIM divisor, fuel base, and accessorial schedules. We don't guess your discount; we read it from your invoices.
  3. Behavioral rules learn from your historical patterns. If 92% of your overnight shipments to a single lane could have moved on 2-day without missing a deadline, that lane gets a recommendation.

Confidence, not certainty

We never auto-correct. Every flagged shipment surfaces with a confidence score and the underlying reasoning, so your team can verify before acting. A high-confidence flag — say, "this overnight to Brooklyn was unnecessary; ground would have arrived the same day" — is a clear win. A medium-confidence flag — "this lane is consistently overspending on Saturday delivery" — is a conversation to have with the team that books it.

Why this works without integrations

Because we ingest your invoices and tracking data directly from the carriers, we don't need to touch your TMS, ERP, or WMS. The first dollar of waste is identified within hours of connecting your carrier accounts. The first conversation with operations usually happens within the first week.

What it looks like in practice

One enterprise retailer found that 14% of their air shipments to the West Coast could have moved on 2-day ground without affecting customer-promised dates. Another discovered they were paying for residential surcharges on 8% of B2B shipments because the addresses had been miscoded. A third surfaced a $400K annual leak on a single accessorial — a charge their carrier had quietly added six months earlier.

None of these were visible in their existing reporting. They were hiding in the shape of the data.

What's next

We're working on a few things: better lane clustering, automatic detection of new accessorials carriers add mid-contract, and a rules editor so customers can codify their own internal policies into the engine. If there's a pattern you'd like flagged that we don't catch today, tell us.
